# Computational-Study-of-Family-Discourse-in-Stalinist-Adult-Drama-1945-1953

## Overview

This repository contains research materials accompanying a DH2026 (Daejeon, 2026)
presentation on the ideological functions of family and kinship in
late Stalinist Soviet drama.

The study examines a corpus of 46 plays written and staged between
1945 and 1953 and combines corpus construction, TEI encoding,
character annotation, lexical analysis, and topic modelling.

## Research questions

- How does late Stalinist drama transform family from a private
  relation into an ideological model of social organisation?
- How does kinship vocabulary vary across ideological character types?
- How do male and female characters differ in their use of kinship
  and institutional vocabulary?
- How is family discourse embedded within broader thematic structures?

## Corpus

The corpus consists of 46 plays for adult audiences written and
staged between 1945 and 1953.

The texts were digitised from printed editions, corrected,
encoded according to TEI principles, and lemmatised.

The original corpus consists of 46 Soviet plays published between 1945 and 1953. The full texts are not included in this repository due to copyright restrictions.

## Methods

The analysis combines:

- TEI-based corpus construction
- manual character annotation
- TF-IDF analysis
- gender-based Z-score analysis
- topic modelling
